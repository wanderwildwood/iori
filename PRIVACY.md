# Privacy

Home collects nothing about you and has no analytics, ads or tracking of any kind. It has no
server of its own.

- **Your layout, settings, usage counts and everything shown on home** stay on the phone.
  Android's own backup (to Google) and phone-to-phone transfer take none of it; to move your
  layout, use Back up and restore in Home's settings, which writes a file where you choose.
- **Notifications**, when you give Home notification access, are read on the phone for badges,
  Letters and the music widget, and kept only there.
- **Your apps** reads the list of the house's apps from wanderthe.dev and downloads from GitHub
  the ones you ask for, only from wanderwildwood's own releases there. These requests name the
  app only as "Home", not the phone's model or Android build.
- **Nextcloud**: if you sign in, Home talks only to the server you named, over https, to sign in
  and to sign out. The app password is kept locked on the phone and handed only to the apps of
  this family that use it, checked by their signing key.
- **Home lines** come from Glance on the phone and do not leave it.
- **Crash reports** are sent only if you choose to, as an email from your own mail app. They hold
  the error, Home's version, the app it was installed from, and the phone's maker, model, Android
  version and processor type: nothing about the apps you use.
- **Accessibility** (Home actions) stays off until you turn it on. It is used for the gesture
  actions and the lock-screen music strip, and watches only the lock screen.
- **Terms:** free software under the GNU General Public License v3 only, with no warranty.

## Who we are

Home is made by wander wildwood, an independent developer: one person, not a company. That makes
wander wildwood the data controller for anything the app itself handles, and that is very
little: your layout and settings stay on this phone unless you send a backup somewhere yourself,
and nothing comes to the developer.

## How to contact us

Write to email@wanderthe.dev about your data, this policy or anything else. The same address is
in the app, under About Home.

## Your rights

If you are in the EU, the EEA or the UK, the GDPR gives you the right to:

- see the personal data held about you (access)
- have it corrected (rectification) or deleted (erasure)
- limit how it is used (restriction)
- object to its use (objection)
- get a copy to take elsewhere (portability)
- withdraw your consent at any time, where something rests on it
- complain to a data protection supervisory authority, for example the one where you live

Similar laws elsewhere, such as the CCPA in California, give you the right to know, delete and
correct, and to opt out of the sale or sharing of your data. Nothing from Home is sold or shared.

Since Home keeps what it stores on your phone, you can exercise most of these rights yourself:
clear the app's data in Android's settings, or uninstall the app, which deletes all of it. For
anything you cannot do yourself, write to the address above.

When you open Your apps or get an app, Home reaches wanderthe.dev, which GitHub Pages serves,
and GitHub. Like any website, they see your phone's internet address; GitHub's own privacy policy
(https://docs.github.com/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement) covers
what it does with it. If you sign in to a Nextcloud of your own, what that server keeps is yours
to manage.
